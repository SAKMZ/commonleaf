The name comes from the *stoa poikile*, the painted porch in Athens where Zeno
taught. It is worth remembering that the school was named after a place people
stood around in, not after a doctrine.

## The dichotomy of control

Epictetus opens the *Enchiridion* with the whole of it: some things are up to
us and some are not. Almost everything that follows is a consequence.

> [!quote] Epictetus, Enchiridion I
> Of things some are in our power, and others are not. In our power are
> opinion, movement towards a thing, desire, aversion.

What makes this useful rather than merely consoling is that it is a question
you can actually ask in the moment — *is this mine to decide?* — and get an
answer.

## Where it connects

The practice is closer to [[Atomic Habits]] than the reputation suggests: both
treat character as the residue of repeated action.
